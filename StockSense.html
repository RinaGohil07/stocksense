Why

    All prices here are simulated so the demo works offline. Company fundamentals are illustrative sample values. This is a learning project, not investment advice.

    What is inside

    One system. Six modules.

    Each part of StockSense does one job and can be improved without touching the rest.

    Data

    Data collector

    Loads daily open, high, low, close and volume for each stock from a market data source.

    Prepare

    Cleaner

    Fills missing days, checks for odd values, and scales prices so the model can learn from them.

    Indicators

    Indicator engine

    Calculates moving averages, RSI and trend strength, the signals a trader would read by eye.

    Predict

    Forecast model

    Projects the next 7, 14 or 30 days and draws the range the price is likely to stay within.

    Score

    Score engine

    Combines four signals into one score from 0 to 100, weighted for how far ahead you are looking.

    Show

    Web dashboard

    Shows the score, chart and reasons together, so every result can be checked and questioned.

    Look at the market first

    A good stock in a bad market is still risky.

    Before it scores any single stock, StockSense checks the mood of the wider market. If the market is weak, the score is held back, and the reasons say so.

    The mood is worked out from how an index is moving against its own 50-day average.

    How the score is built

    Four signals, weighted for your horizon.

    Every stock is scored on technical trend, momentum, market mood and fundamentals. For a 7-day view, price action matters most. For a 30-day view, the company's own strength counts for more.

    This is why one stock can score differently when you change how far ahead you look.

    The pipeline

    From raw prices to a forecast.

    1. Collect

      Price history is loaded for the chosen stock.

    2. Clean

      Gaps and outliers are handled and values are scaled.

    3. Analyse

      Indicators turn prices into readable signals.

    4. Predict

      The model projects forward and shows a range.

    Technology

    Built with.

    Data

    Python, Pandas and NumPy for loading, cleaning and features. Prices from a source such as Yahoo Finance.

    Models

    Linear regression and moving-average rules as the baseline, with an LSTM neural network planned for deeper trend learning.

    Evaluation

    Scikit-learn metrics such as RMSE and MAE on recent held-out data, so forecasts are scored against what happened.

    Web

    HTML, CSS and JavaScript on the front end, with Flask or FastAPI serving predictions from the Python models.

    Questions, answered

    What is a stock?

    A stock (or share) is a small piece of ownership in a company. If the company grows and earns more, the value of its shares can rise. Some companies also pay part of their profit to shareholders as a dividend. If the company does badly, the share price can fall, and you can lose money.

    What is the stock market?

    It is a marketplace where people buy and sell shares of listed companies. In India the two main exchanges are the NSE and the BSE. Prices move all day based on demand and supply: when more people want to buy a stock than sell it, the price goes up, and the other way round.

    What are Sensex and Nifty 50?

    They are indexes, which are single numbers that show how the market as a whole is doing. Sensex follows 30 large companies on the BSE, and Nifty 50 follows 50 large companies on the NSE. When the index is rising, the broad market is usually in a good mood.

    What is a bull market and a bear market?

    In a bull market prices are rising broadly and people feel confident. In a bear market prices are falling broadly, often by 20% or more from a recent high, and people feel fearful. StockSense reads this mood before it scores any single stock.

    What is a candlestick?

    A candlestick is a way to draw one day of trading. The thick body shows where the price opened and closed, and the thin lines show the highest and lowest prices of the day. The faint candles in the top of this page are only decoration.

    What is a moving average?

    It is the average of a stock's last few closing prices, for example the last 20 days. It smooths out daily ups and downs so the trend is easier to see. When the 20-day average is above the 50-day average, the stock has recently been rising faster than its longer trend, which many traders read as an uptrend.

    What is RSI?

    The Relative Strength Index is a number from 0 to 100 that measures how fast a price has risen or fallen recently. A common rule of thumb says above 70 means overbought, so a pullback becomes more likely, and below 30 means oversold, so a bounce becomes more likely. It is a hint, not a guarantee.

    How does StockSense score a stock?

    It scores four signals from 0 to 100: technical trend, momentum, market mood and company fundamentals. These are blended into one score using weights that depend on how far ahead you look. For a 7-day view, trend and momentum matter most. For a 30-day view, company strength counts for more. A score of 65 or more is Bullish, 40 or less is Bearish, and anything between is Neutral.

    How does the forecast work?

    The current model fits a straight trend line to the last 30 days of prices and projects it forward. It also draws a shaded range, so you see how uncertain the forecast is instead of a single number. This is the baseline model. A neural network such as an LSTM is planned as the next step.

    What is the market mood?

    It is a quick health check of the wider market. StockSense compares an index with its own 50-day average and looks at its recent move. The result is Green, Mixed or Red. If the mood is Red, the verdict for any stock is held back from Bullish, because even good stocks struggle in a falling market.

    Is the data on this page real?

    No. The prices in the demo are simulated so that the page works offline and always looks the same. The company figures, such as return on equity and growth, are sample values. The final version is designed to load real historical prices from a market data source.

    How accurate are the predictions?

    No model can predict a stock price exactly. Prices are moved by news, company results, government decisions, global events and the behaviour of other investors, and a model that only sees past prices cannot know any of these. That is why StockSense shows a likely range, and why forecasts should be tested on recent data using measures such as RMSE and MAE.

    Which technologies are used?

    Python with Pandas and NumPy for data, scikit-learn for regression and evaluation, and an LSTM neural network in TensorFlow or PyTorch for the advanced model. The website uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and Flask or FastAPI can serve predictions from the Python models.

    What could be added in future?

    Live price data, an LSTM model compared against the baseline, news sentiment from headlines, back-testing to check how past signals performed, a larger list of stocks, and user accounts with saved watchlists.

    Is StockSense investment advice?

    No. StockSense is a student project made for learning. Its scores and forecasts are statistical estimates and should not be used to decide what to buy or sell. Anyone investing real money should do their own research or speak to a registered financial advisor.
